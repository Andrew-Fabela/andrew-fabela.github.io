The Craft — Design Work

Design work, built by hand.

Trained in Stanford's Design Engineering program — where engineering rigor meets human-centered design. Selected work below.

Mechanical Design · ME 102

Tik-Tok

A fully functional timepiece powered by nothing but a falling weight. As gravity pulls the weight down, a stamp on a vertical linkage rocks between an ink pad and a moving paper belt — printing a physical record of the passage of time, one stamp at a time.

Built with a five-person team in Stanford's ME 102: a gear train of 3D-printed and laser-cut parts on an acrylic frame, iterated until the whole mechanism ran reliably on gravitational potential energy alone.

Product Realization · ME 103

JawDrop

A custom-machined aluminum cup holder adapter that lets any bottle or can sit securely in a car. Three powder-coated jaws, spaced 120° apart, grip anything cylindrical. The base drops into the existing cup holder — no tools, no modifications.

Every part built by hand from 6061 aluminum in Stanford's Product Realization Lab — plates turned on the lathe, jaw slots indexed 120° apart on the mill's rotary table, brackets waterjet-cut and formed, then powder coated in red and gold. Presented at Meet the Makers, March 2026.
